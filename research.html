Research

My research focuses on developing computational techniques for modelling electromagnetic systems interacting with nonlinear electronic circuits, with applications in RF and microwave engineering.

Research Vision

Modern communication and electronic systems increasingly involve strong interactions between electromagnetic fields and nonlinear electronic circuits. Conventional electromagnetic and circuit simulation tools often analyse these domains separately, resulting in reduced accuracy for broadband and nonlinear problems.

My research aims to bridge this gap through computationally efficient hybrid simulation techniques that combine full-wave electromagnetic analysis with nonlinear circuit modelling, enabling accurate prediction of complex RF system behaviour.

Research Themes

Computational Electromagnetics

Numerical methods for solving complex electromagnetic problems using time-domain techniques.

Hybrid FDTD–SPICE

Coupled electromagnetic–circuit simulation for nonlinear RF and microwave systems.

Nonlinear Electromagnetic Systems

Analysis of nonlinear interactions under broadband excitation including OFDM signals.

RF & Microwave Engineering

Passive microwave structures, antennas, measurement techniques and device characterisation.

Current Research

My doctoral research is centred on the development of a hybrid FDTD–SPICE simulation framework for analysing broadband electromagnetic systems containing nonlinear electronic components.

The framework enables arbitrary excitation waveforms to be directly incorporated into full-wave electromagnetic simulations, allowing accurate time-domain prediction of nonlinear system behaviour.

Experimental validation is carried out using microwave hardware, software-defined radios, and spectrum analysis techniques.
